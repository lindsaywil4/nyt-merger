Where the merger adds trains in Houston

The railroads' own segment tables show the merger adding trains and freight tonnage on rail lines across Houston. The federal environmental review studies only three of those segments, and all three end at Tower 87 on the northeast side. Every segment through the East End, Fifth Ward, Downtown and the Near Northside is left out.

How to read the map

  • Tap any colored line to see its trains per day today, five years out without the merger, and five years out with it.
  • Blue lines are the only Houston-area segments the Surface Transportation Board's environmental office will study. They qualify because the merger adds 5 trains a day, above the trigger of 3.
  • Red lines add trains but fall under the trigger, so they get no study. Most add about 2 trains a day, roughly one train short of the cutoff.
  • Purple dashed lines carry more freight weight on the same number of trains. Heavier trains are usually longer trains. These segments can never meet the train-count trigger.
  • Each segment is judged on its own. No table adds up the trains across neighboring segments.
  • Outlined areas are the City of Houston super neighborhoods that make up the East End and Fifth Ward. Nearby neighborhoods are labeled in lighter type.
  • Orange dots (off by default) show 2025 blocked-crossing reports filed on the Federal Railroad Administration's public portal. Bigger dots mean more reports. Eastwood Street alone drew 908, the most of any crossing in the country.
  • Green squares (off by default) show public schools, including charters. Use the legend to turn either layer on.

About these numbers

  • Train counts and tonnage come from the railroads' September 9, 2026 master segment tables (EI-34309), the latest version filed with the STB's Office of Environmental Analysis. Trains per day include local trains. Houston train counts are identical in the April 30, July 27 and September 9 versions.
  • "Tons" is the merger's change in gross ton-miles, as a share of the Year 5 no-merger level. The environmental trigger for these lines is 3 added trains a day or a 50% tonnage increase.
  • Segment lines are drawn along the national rail network (USDOT/BTS North American Rail Network) between endpoints placed from the filings' mileposts. Endpoints are approximate, especially on short yard connections south of Downtown.
  • OEA's own online map still shows January 2026 figures from the rejected application. This page uses the current filing.
  • None of the filings states a projected train length for any Houston segment.
  • Blocked-crossing reports are voluntary public complaints, not sensor readings. Counts are calendar year 2025 after removing exact duplicate records, matched to crossing locations in the federal grade crossing inventory.
